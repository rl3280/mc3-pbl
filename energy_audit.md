# 🥚 Project Design Outline: The Urban Energy Easter Egg Hunt

### **Project Overview**

* **Grade Level & Subject:** 12th Grade / Green Building & Energy Audit
* **Duration:** 1 Week (Low-stress, asynchronous fieldwork)
* **Group Structure:** Pairs
* **Core Task:** Walk the city, spot energy waste or opportunities, snap a photo, and run quick calculations showing energy reduction under a LEED credit.

---

## 1. Challenging Problem or Question

* **Driving Question:** *What hidden energy waste can you spot on your daily walk, and how much power could we save by fixing it?*
* **Scenario / Context:** Students treat their neighborhood or daily commute like a real-world scavenger hunt. As they walk around, they hunt for building features wasting energy (uninsulated brick walls, bare flat roofs, single-pane glass, ancient HVAC units) and figure out how to "retrofit" them on the spot.

---

## 2. Sustained Inquiry & Milestones

This is an informal, student-driven inquiry experience rather than a strict daily regimen:

* **The Walk (Fieldwork):** Pairs go for a walk outside in their neighborhood, commute, or city block to hunt for candidate buildings.
* **The "Two Things" Milestone:** Students capture just **two core deliverables**:
1. A photograph of the building/feature wasting energy.
2. A quick back-of-the-envelope math calculation showing the energy savings (BTU / kWh) if retrofitted.



---

## 3. Authenticity

* **Real-World Connection:** Direct, hands-on exploration of climate change solutions. Instead of reading about emissions in a textbook, students physically see where fossil fuels are being burned unnecessarily right outside their front door.
* **Real Audience:** The public online community visiting the class Padlet wall.

---

## 4. Student Voice & Choice

* **Total Freedom of Route:** Students choose where they walk, which building catches their eye, and what picture they take.
* **Technology Focus:** They pick whatever energy-saving angle interests them—solar panels, green roofs, insulation upgrades, greywater systems, or efficient windows.

---

## 5. Public Product

* **Final Deliverable:** A single, casual **Padlet Post** per pair containing:
* **The Photo:** Visual evidence of the building site/feature.
* **The Math & LEED Connection:** A straightforward calculation connecting a LEED credit to reduced energy use.
* *Example 1:* Insulation added to 4 bare walls --> Saves **$0.8\text{ MBTU/hr}$** (LEED Optimize Energy Performance).
* *Example 2:* Solar cells added to a flat roof --> Generates **$3\text{ MWh}$** (LEED Renewable Energy).



---

## 6. Critique & Revision

* **Peer Feedback:** Keep it light and interactive. Students scroll through the Padlet wall like a social feed and leave quick comments or questions on each other’s posts.

---

## 7. Reflection

* **Informal Wrap-Up:** A quick class debrief or brief comment on the Padlet reflecting on what surprised them most about energy loss in their own neighborhood after taking a closer look.

---

## 🛠️ Success Skills Emphasized

* **Observation & Environmental Awareness:** Training eyes to spot energy waste in everyday urban architecture.
* **Practical Quantitative Skills:** Translating physical retrofits into quick, real-world energy reduction math ($\text{BTUs}$ and $\text{kWh}$).
* **Peer Engagement:** Sharing local observations casually via digital tools.

---

## 📌 Constraints & Resources

* **Boundary:** Building must be along the student's actual neighborhood, community, or commute route.
* **Requirement:** Must tie the math back to a relevant LEED credit/concept.
* **Platform:** [Class Padlet Board](https://padlet.com/teacherscollege120/calculating-carbon-footprint-reductions-d2c19gjryvxplllh).
